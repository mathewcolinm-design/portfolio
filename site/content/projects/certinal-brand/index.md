## Overview

Certinal is an eSignature and compliance company. In 2026 the CEO wanted to move the brand on from its old look. I worked closely with the CEO and the team to find the new direction, then built the whole identity out from it: the logo kit, the brand deck, the website and the event booths.

## The challenge

The old logo was a hexagon around a shield with a bold CERTINAL wordmark, in solid green with a small gradient on the shield. It was recognisable, but it was a mark, not a system: there was nothing in it to build a brand from.

The new identity had to keep what people already knew about Certinal, and give marketing a visual language it could use across hundreds of assets without everything starting from scratch.

## How I approached it

### Exploring directions with the CEO
I explored four brand directions and reviewed them with the CEO and the team, narrowing down until we agreed on one idea: layers.

### Keeping the shape people knew
The hexagon, the shield and the bold caps stayed. What changed is the fill: the solid green became six greens stacked as layered rings, running from deep green to lime.

### Turning the logo into a kit
I built a logo kit that leaves nothing to guesswork. Three lockups: horizontal (the default), stacked and the icon. Each comes in full colour, black and white, plus app icons on white, black and green tiles. Spacing is measured in X, the height of the capitals: the caps sit centred on the icon, which overhangs them by 0.33X, and the gap to the C is 0.42X. The kit also sets clear space, minimum sizes, the don'ts, and one naming pattern for every file.

### Building the brand from the layers
Once the logo was settled, every other asset grew from its layered rings: the colour palette, backgrounds, the brand deck, website graphics and the event booths.

### Scaling it with AI
The website needed far more graphics than one designer could draw by hand. I built an AI-assisted design workflow in Figma that produced on-brand assets at scale, so all of them followed the same layered system.

## Design decisions

- **One palette, six greens.** From deep #01B46D to lime #CDF757. The logo uses the palette as it is and is never recoloured.
- **Colour by background.** Full colour on white and light neutrals, black on lime, and white on green, black, dark imagery and video. The colour logo disappears on green.
- **Small sizes go single-colour.** At 32px and below the layered rings blur, so the kit switches to the black or white icon, or an app tile.

## The outcome

Certinal now has one layered identity across its logo, brand deck, website and event presence. The workflow delivered more than 850 on-brand website assets across 13 sections of the site, all from the same system.
