# Certinal: rebranding an eSignature company

From one flat logo to a layered identity. Brand & creative designer, Jun 2026 – Nov 2026.

**In numbers:** 850+ on-brand website assets · 13 website sections · 12 logo artworks

## Where it started

Certinal is an eSignature and compliance company. By 2026 its logo had been the same for years: a hexagon around a shield, with a bold CERTINAL wordmark in solid green. It was recognisable, but it was only ever a logo. There was nothing in it to build a brand from.

The CEO wanted a new brand. I worked closely with the CEO and the team to find it, then built the identity out from there.

## The brief

- **Keep the recognition.** Customers already knew the hexagon and the shield, so the new brand couldn't throw them away.
- **Give it a system.** Marketing needed a visual language that could stretch across hundreds of assets without being reinvented each time.
- **Make it hold up everywhere.** The same brand had to work on a favicon, a website, a sales deck and an event booth.

## Finding the idea

### Directions with the CEO
I explored four brand directions and reviewed them with the CEO and the team, and together we chose one idea: layers.

### Same shape, new depth
The hexagon, the shield and the bold caps stayed. What changed is the fill: one flat green became six greens stacked as layered rings, running from deep green at the outer edge to lime at the centre. The logo people knew is still there; it just has depth now.

## Building the mark

### Measured, not eyeballed
Every lockup is built on X, the height of the capitals. The caps sit centred on the icon, which overhangs them by 0.33X above and below, and the gap from the icon to the C is 0.42X. The horizontal and stacked logos use the same construction, so they read as one family.

### A full logo kit
Three lockups (horizontal by default, stacked for square spaces, and the icon on its own), each in full colour, black and white, plus app icons on white, black and green tiles. The kit sets clear space, minimum sizes and the don'ts, and gives every file one naming pattern so nobody picks the wrong one.

## Colour and pattern

### Six greens, two neutrals
The palette is the logo itself: #01B46D through to #CDF757, with white and black as the neutrals. The logo is never recoloured; instead the background picks the version. Full colour on white, black on lime, and white on green, black or imagery.

### A pattern from the logo
The rings inside the icon became the brand's graphic. Cropped into a corner, rising like a horizon or drawn as fine lines, the same rings carry the brand on surfaces where the logo itself would be too much.

### Small sizes go simple
At 32px and below the layered rings blur, so the kit switches to the single-colour icon or an app tile.

## Taking it everywhere

### The website
The website needed far more graphics than one designer could draw by hand. I built an AI-assisted design workflow in Figma that produced on-brand assets at scale, all built from the same layered system. It delivered more than 850 assets across 13 sections of certinal.com.

### Decks and events
From there the identity went into the brand deck and Certinal's event booths, each built from the logo, the six greens and the rings. The stationery, social profile and event pass mockups show how the same three pieces extend to anything else the brand needs.

## The outcome

Certinal went from a single flat logo to a complete identity: one layered mark, a measured logo kit, a palette taken straight from the logo, and a pattern that ties everything together. The same system now runs across the website, the brand deck and Certinal's events.
